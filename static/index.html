Scientific limitations

The saved hybrid benchmark includes 18 held-out synthetic episodes per policy but does not establish quantum advantage. Selective QAOA reduced shots relative to always QAOA; classical-only achieved the same final outcome with zero shots. The random baseline was calibrated on development episodes but does not have an exactly matched held-out call count. Planning uses a single-browser demo budget, not a secure server-wide quota. Shaded moisture chart bands are scenario percentiles, not statistical confidence intervals.

METHODOLOGY

Research principles

What JalQ-R computes, what is genuinely quantum, and what the prototype cannot claim.

01 / MODEL

Exactly-K scheduling

Three fields, four windows, and an equal-volume allocation. Completed events are frozen. The planner uses current observations and forecast scenarios, never realized future weather.

02 / QUANTUM

Expected-stress surrogate

A quadratic Hamiltonian uses RZ/RZZ phase rotations and XX+YY constraint-preserving mixing. Budgeted COBYLA tunes the circuit angles. Quantum measurements generate candidates; fractional-tail CVaR ranking is performed classically.

03 / CONTRIBUTION

Selective invocation

Our primary comparison is selective QAOA versus always-run QAOA: equal shot allocations per invocation, fewer calls across rolling weather updates. Forecast shifts, regret and candidate instability control invocation; the gate does not predict quantum advantage.

04 / EVIDENCE

Separated outcomes

We report quantum samples independently from final schedules protected by classical fallback. Exact enumeration audits saved plans, and classical-only remains visible. Fewer quantum shots than always QAOA is not superiority to classical-only, hardware speedup or water savings.
